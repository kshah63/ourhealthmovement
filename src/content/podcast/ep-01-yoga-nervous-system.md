In our first episode we get underneath the poses. Yoga teacher and breath educator **Maya
Ellison** joins us to talk about the part of yoga that changed her life long before she
could touch her toes: the nervous system.

We get into why the exhale is the body's brake pedal, how just a few minutes of slow
breathing shifts you out of fight-or-flight, and how to build tiny, repeatable practices
that fit a busy life. It's a warm, practical conversation for anyone who's ever thought
they were "too stiff" or "too busy" for yoga.

## In this episode

- **(02:10)** What drew Maya from athletics to yoga — and the burnout that started it
- **(11:40)** The eight limbs: why asana is the tip of the iceberg
- **(19:05)** The vagus nerve, explained simply — and why the exhale matters most
- **(28:30)** A 3-minute breath practice you can do at your desk
- **(37:15)** "Steady and at ease" — reframing effort, on and off the mat
- **(46:00)** Maya's one small daily habit for a calmer baseline

## Try it yourself

The practice Maya teaches around **(28:30)** is simple: breathe in for a count of four,
out for a count of six, for ten rounds. The longer exhale is what does the work. Read more
in our guide to [Yoga](/wisdom/yoga).

> "You don't calm down and then breathe slowly. You breathe slowly, and *then* you calm
> down. The body leads." — Maya Ellison
