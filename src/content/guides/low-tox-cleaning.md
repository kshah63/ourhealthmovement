## Three staples do most of the work

The cleaning aisle sells complexity; your grandparents' cupboard sold results. Most
day-to-day cleaning comes down to three inexpensive, low-tox staples:

- **White vinegar**, cuts grease and limescale, and shines glass.
- **Bicarbonate of soda (baking soda)**, a gentle abrasive and deodoriser.
- **Castile soap**, a plant-oil soap for general washing.

Add a microfibre cloth and hot water and you can clean most of a home.

## Simple recipes

- **All-purpose spray:** equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle, with a
  few drops of essential oil if you like. *(Not for stone worktops, vinegar etches
  marble and granite; use castile soap and water there instead.)*
- **Gentle scrub:** sprinkle bicarb, spray with a little vinegar, let it fizz, then scour.
  Great for sinks, tubs and ovens.
- **Glass & mirrors:** vinegar-water and a microfibre cloth, buffed dry. No streaks, no
  fragrance.

## What to move away from

- **"Air fresheners" and plug-ins.** These add fragrance chemicals to the air you breathe
  all day. **Open a window**, or simmer citrus peel and spices on the stove instead.
- **Antibacterial everything.** For a normal home, routine disinfecting of every surface
  isn't necessary, and plain cleaning removes most of what matters.
- **Heavily fragranced laundry products.** Fragrance lingers on the clothes and bedding
  against your skin all night. A fragrance-free detergent is a quiet, high-impact swap.

## Ventilation is free, and underrated

Whatever you clean with, **air the house**. Opening windows for a few minutes clears
indoor pollutants better than any spray promising "fresh." It costs nothing and is the
most effective low-tox habit of all.
