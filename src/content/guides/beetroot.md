## In season: autumn into winter

As the year turns inward, the harvest turns to **roots** — foods that grow in the dark,
store for months, and carry us through winter. Beetroot is a cornerstone: sweet, earthy
and available locally long after the summer crops have gone.

## Why it fits the season

Autumn and winter, across traditions, call for **warming, grounding, building** foods —
and roots are exactly that. Ayurveda values beetroot as blood-building and strengthening;
folk tradition has long linked its deep red to vitality. Modern research points to its
**dietary nitrates**, which the body converts to nitric oxide — relaxing blood vessels and
supporting healthy blood pressure and stamina.

## Choosing & keeping

- Pick beets that are **firm and smooth**, with fresh green tops if attached (a good
  freshness sign — and the leaves are edible, like chard).
- Twist off the tops before storing so they don't draw moisture from the root.
- They keep for **weeks** in the fridge drawer — one of the great virtues of a root.

## Simple ways to enjoy it

- **Roasted whole** in their skins until tender, then slipped out of their jackets and
  dressed with oil, vinegar and thyme.
- **Grated raw** into a slaw with apple and carrot for brightness and crunch.
- **Blended** into a warming soup with cumin and a swirl of yoghurt.

> Wear an apron and rinse boards quickly — beetroot's colour is glorious and gets
> everywhere. (The pink is harmless, even if it's briefly alarming the next morning.)
