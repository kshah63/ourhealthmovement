## In season: winter

It's easy to think of oranges as a summer fruit, but citrus is a **winter crop** — trees
fruiting through the coldest, darkest months. There's a lovely logic to it: just as fresh
green vegetables grow scarce, the season delivers a burst of **vitamin C** and colour.
Blood oranges, satsumas, lemons and grapefruit are all at their best now.

## Why it fits the season

Winter cooking leans warm and heavy; citrus brings the **brightness and lift** that keeps
it balanced. Its **vitamin C** supports the immune system through cold season, while the
sourness stimulates digestion and cuts through rich food. In TCM, the sour flavour is
associated with gathering and astringing — a fitting note for the year's most inward
season.

## Choosing & keeping

- Choose fruit that feels **heavy for its size** — that's juice — with taut, fragrant skin.
- Store at room temperature for a week, or in the fridge for longer.
- **Don't waste the zest.** The peel holds intensely aromatic oils; zest before you juice
  and freeze what you don't use.

## Simple ways to enjoy it

- **Sliced blood oranges** with a little olive oil, salt and mint — a stunning winter
  salad.
- **Warm water with lemon** to start the day, an old habit for gently waking digestion.
- **Zest into everything** — dressings, yoghurt, cakes, roasted veg — for brightness
  without extra sugar or salt.

> Roll a lemon or orange firmly under your palm before juicing — it breaks the internal
> membranes and you'll get noticeably more juice.
