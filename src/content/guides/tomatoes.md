## In season: summer

There is no better argument for seasonal, local eating than a tomato. Ripened on the vine
in summer sun, it's sweet, fragrant and juicy; picked green and shipped in winter, it's
pale and mealy. Buy them **in high summer**, from a market or your own pots, and you'll
never go back.

## Why it fits the season

Summer runs hot, *Pitta* season in Ayurveda, the Fire element in TCM, and asks for
**cooling, hydrating** foods. Tomatoes fit exactly: over 90% water, faintly sour, and
refreshing raw. They're a leading source of **lycopene**, an antioxidant linked to heart
and skin health, and, helpfully, one the body absorbs *better* when tomatoes are cooked
with a little oil.

## Choosing & keeping

- Choose tomatoes that are **heavy for their size** and smell sweet at the stem.
- **Never refrigerate** ripe tomatoes, cold flattens their flavour and turns the flesh
  mealy. Keep them stem-side down on the counter.
- Slightly underripe? A day or two in a paper bag with a banana will bring them along.

## Simple ways to enjoy it

- **Just sliced**, with good salt, olive oil and torn basil, the whole summer on a plate.
- **Panzanella**: ripe tomatoes, torn bread, cucumber, red onion and plenty of oil.
- **Slow-roasted** with garlic and herbs to concentrate the sweetness, and unlock more
  lycopene.

> A pinch of salt ten minutes before serving draws out the juices and deepens the flavour
> of even a good tomato.
