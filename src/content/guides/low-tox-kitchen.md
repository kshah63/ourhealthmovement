## Start where you cook

"Low-tox" isn't about fear or perfection, it's about **reducing the small, daily
exposures** you have easy control over. The kitchen, where heat meets food meets storage,
is the highest-leverage room in the house. You don't need to replace everything at once.
Swap things **as they wear out**, and start with what you use most.

## The swaps worth making

| Swap this | For this | Why it's worth it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scratched non-stick pans | **Cast iron or stainless steel** | Damaged non-stick coatings can shed into food when overheated. Well-seasoned cast iron lasts generations. |
| Plastic storage tubs | **Glass jars & containers** | Plastics can leach into fatty or acidic food, more so when heated. Glass is inert and lasts forever. |
| Reheating in plastic | **A plate or glass** | Never microwave food in plastic; heat is what drives migration into food. |
| Plastic chopping boards | **Wood or bamboo** | Wood is naturally antimicrobial and doesn't shed micro-plastic with every cut. |
| Cling film | **Beeswax wraps or a plate on a bowl** | Reusable, and no plastic touching your food. |
| Plastic utensils in hot pans | **Wood or stainless** | Hot oil plus soft plastic is the worst-case combination. |

## Two more that punch above their weight

- **Filter your water.** A simple carbon filter reduces chlorine and some contaminants,
  and makes tap water taste better than bottled, with none of the plastic.
- **Buy loose, store in glass.** Decanting dry goods into jars cuts packaging, keeps food
  fresher, and makes a pantry you actually want to cook from.

## Keep it in perspective

The goal is **direction, not purity**. Every swap you make is a small reduction that
compounds over the years, and most of these are cheaper and more durable than what they
replace. Progress beats perfection every time.
