## In season: spring

Asparagus has one of the shortest, most celebrated seasons of any vegetable — a matter of
weeks in late spring. That brevity is the point: eating it *now*, from a local grower, is
seasonal eating at its most rewarding. Out of season it's flown across the world and a
shadow of itself.

## Why it fits the season

Spring, in both Ayurveda and TCM, is a time of **gentle lightening** after the heavier
food of winter. Asparagus is perfect for it — light, faintly bitter, and traditionally
considered cleansing and diuretic. It's rich in **folate, vitamin K** and **inulin**, a
prebiotic fibre that feeds the gut bacteria we're increasingly learning to look after.

## Choosing & keeping

- Look for **firm, straight spears** with tight, dry tips. Limp or slimy tips mean it's
  past its best.
- Store it like flowers: stand the stems in a little water in the fridge, or wrap the ends
  in a damp cloth. Eat within a couple of days.
- **Thin vs. thick** is about preference, not quality — thick spears are meatier, thin
  ones cook in seconds.

## Simple ways to enjoy it

- **Roasted or griddled** with olive oil and salt until just tender, with a squeeze of
  lemon — the tips going crisp.
- **Barely blanched** and dressed while warm, so it keeps its snap.
- **Folded through** a spring risotto, frittata or grain bowl at the very end.

> Snap, don't cut: hold each spear and bend — it breaks naturally where the woody end
> gives way to the tender stalk.
