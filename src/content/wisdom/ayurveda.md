## The oldest continuous system of natural health

Ayurveda, literally *the knowledge (veda) of life (ayus)*, is among the world's oldest
recorded approaches to wellbeing, refined in India over thousands of years. Its genius is
not a list of superfoods but a **framework**: understand your own constitution, notice
where you tend to fall out of balance, and use food, routine and the qualities of the
season to bring yourself back.

### Reading yourself in the elements

Ayurveda describes everything (bodies, foods, weather, emotions) through five elements
that cluster into three *doshas*:

- **Vata**: air and space. Movement, creativity, quickness. Out of balance: anxious,
  dry, scattered, cold.
- **Pitta**: fire and water. Focus, drive, digestion. Out of balance: irritable,
  inflamed, overheated.
- **Kapha**: earth and water. Steadiness, strength, calm. Out of balance: heavy,
  sluggish, congested.

You are born with a natural mix (*prakriti*) and drift into a current state (*vikriti*).
Health is the art of noticing the drift early and answering it with the **opposite
quality**: warmth for cold, lightness for heaviness, routine for restlessness.

### Where ancient meets modern

Much of what Ayurveda intuited maps onto ideas we study today: the importance of
**circadian rhythm** (its daily and seasonal routines), the **gut as the root of health**
(agni and the microbiome), and the anti-inflammatory potential of culinary spices like
**turmeric and ginger**. You don't have to take it on faith: you can cook with it,
notice how you feel, and let your own body be the evidence.

> Start small: a warm breakfast, a walk after dinner, and one spice used with intention.
> That's Ayurveda in practice.
