## The practice beneath the poses

In the West, "yoga" often means a mat and a sequence of shapes. In its origins it is
something far wider. The word comes from the Sanskrit root *yuj* — *to yoke, to unite* —
the same ancient root that gives us the English "yoke." What yoga sets out to unite is you
with yourself: body with breath, breath with mind, and the restless everyday self with
something quieter and deeper. One classic description puts it beautifully — yoga as *the
journey of the self, through the self, to the self.* Patanjali later mapped that journey as
**eight limbs** — ethics, self-discipline, posture, breath, withdrawal of the senses,
concentration, meditation and absorption. Asana, the physical poses, is just one.

### Breath: the lever you always have

Of all yoga's tools, **the breath** may be the most quietly powerful. Slow, extended
exhalations tone the *vagus nerve* and tip the nervous system toward "rest and digest."
This is one place where ancient practice and modern physiology shake hands: what yogis
called *pranayama*, researchers now study as breath-based regulation of stress. A minute
of longer exhales can change how you feel — no mat required.

### Fascia: the body's living web

Move through yoga slowly and you're not only stretching muscle — you're hydrating and
mobilising **fascia**, the continuous sheet of connective tissue that wraps every muscle,
bone and organ. Once dismissed as packing material, fascia is now understood as a
sensory, force-transmitting network central to how we move and feel. Long-held, mindful
stretches and varied movement keep it supple — a thread we pull on often, both in the
guides here and on the podcast.

### The layers of a person

Yoga's *koshas* describe five layers, from the physical body outward through the breath,
the mind, wisdom and joy. It's an old way of saying something we're rediscovering: you
can't separate a healthy body from a settled mind and an easy breath. Tend to all of
them, gently, and steadiness follows.

> *Sthira sukham asanam* — steady and at ease. Not a pose to achieve, but a way to meet
> everything you do.
